What I do

  • Run an English lifestyle account every day. Daily posts on X and Threads, mostly about everyday life, with the product showing up on one fixed day a week. A weekly theme for each day (like a Saturday travel series) keeps me from running dry and tells followers what to expect.
  • Talk with real people in their communities. Every day I find English threads about AI companions and reply or send DMs. I check each profile first, stay away from minors and sensitive topics, and don't drop ads into other people's threads.
  • Play by each community's rules. On Reddit I read the rules first and join discussions for real. No vote trading, no accounts boosting each other.
  • Make short promo videos. An English video series built from script and character design to AI-generated reference images and clips, put together five seconds at a time.
  • Pitch features with something you can click. I noticed a daily-reflection habit in how people use the app, built a clickable phone demo of the feature, and pitched it with that instead of a doc.
  • Log everything. What I posted, who I replied to and which angle I used all go into the team's tracking sheet, so we can see which topics actually work.

How I judge whether it works

Not by likes. I care more about which topics bring replies and DMs, because a companion app needs people who want to talk, not people who tap a heart and scroll on.

Case Study

PicWiz cold start in North America

Xuantu AI · Overseas operations · Mar to Jun 2026 · YouTube / TikTok / Instagram, starting from zero followers

The problem

The product's messaging was written for Asian markets. Translated straight into English, it talked about features Western users didn't care about, in a tone that didn't land. The accounts started from zero, with no budget and no existing audience.

What I did

  • Decided again what we were selling. Instead of reusing the old marketing language, I looked at which editing needs Western users actually had and picked features and angles from there.
  • Turned "post and hope" into a weekly decision framework. I broke down user behavior on TikTok, Instagram and YouTube and turned it into a weekly plan: which topics, which hooks, which platform.
  • Ran the whole line myself. Strategy, topics, scripts, filming, editing, publishing and reading the data, with no handoffs.

Results

  • 5,500+ views a week in the first month
  • Best month: 51,000 reach
  • 60+ short videos, 4 over 10K views, top video at 33,100
The part that matters most

For a while, nothing I posted got views

It didn't slowly decline. It just stopped. The easy move is to keep posting and hope the numbers come back. I didn't do that.

1 · Separate the causesSplit it into content, timing and audience instead of blaming luck.
2 · Read my own dataCompare the videos that worked with the ones that didn't. Was the gap in the first three seconds or the topic?
3 · Study othersLook at similar accounts that were growing: how they hook and how they pace.
4 · Change one thing at a timePost again and confirm which change brought views back, so it isn't a lucky guess.

Views came back. I think this says more about how I work than the numbers do: I don't panic when the data is bad, I break the problem apart, and I'm willing to admit my first approach was wrong.
